# Acknowledgements

No book like this starts from nothing. These are the people, works and tools it builds on.

## The people behind the ideas

Every chapter rests on discoveries made over more than two thousand years, by people working on geometry, astronomy, gambling, telegraphs and machines that could learn: Euclid, John Napier, René Descartes, Blaise Pascal and Pierre de Fermat, Isaac Newton and Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, Leonhard Euler, Thomas Bayes and Richard Price, Pierre-Simon Laplace, Carl Friedrich Gauss, Augustin-Louis Cauchy, Arthur Cayley, Andrey Markov, Ronald Fisher, Claude Shannon, and the many researchers in AI whose work is described in the history notes throughout the book. The history notes are short tributes, not complete accounts, and any errors in them are the book's own.

## Books and courses that shaped it

- **Tiago Capelo Monteiro, [*The Math Behind Artificial Intelligence*](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/the-math-behind-artificial-intelligence-book/)** (freeCodeCamp, 2026). Its framing of AI math as four branches (linear algebra, calculus, probability and statistics, optimisation), its engineering perspective on control theory and Kalman filters, and its coverage of determinants, eigenvalues, integrals and Markov models informed several sections of this book. The explanations and examples here are written independently.
- **Grant Sanderson (3Blue1Brown)**, *Essence of Linear Algebra* and *Neural Networks*: the gold standard for seeing mathematics, and an inspiration for the "picture first" approach.
- **Andrej Karpathy**, *Neural Networks: Zero to Hero* and *micrograd*, which inspired the tiny autograd engine in Chapter 4.
- **Marc Peter Deisenroth, A. Aldo Faisal and Cheng Soon Ong**, *Mathematics for Machine Learning*, for readers ready to go deeper.
